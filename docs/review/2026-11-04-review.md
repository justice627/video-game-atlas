# v0.1 review: data and code

Reviewed 2026-11-04 against the v0.1 build on `main`. Since then the data has moved into `data/*.csv` (editable through `data/atlas.xlsx`); each game's flags and notes from this review are in the `flags` and `notes` columns, and flagged games have status `review`. File references below point at the v0.1 code, where `curated.yaml`/`overrides.yaml` and the exact-400 check (C6, now removed) lived. 300 of the 400 games carry at least one flag. Every game is listed in [`data-flags.csv`](data-flags.csv) with its periods, places, flags, notes and source links (Wikidata item and English Wikipedia article), so the data can be reviewed row by row in a spreadsheet.

Method: automated checks over `public/atlas.json`; Wikidata SPARQL for item types and genres; and, for the 45 curated games, the Wikipedia article text checked for each claimed year and place, with every miss read in context. "Unverified" means the article does not confirm the claim. It does not mean the claim is wrong.

## Data issues

Ordered by what to fix first.

### 1. Remove: not games, or not appropriate (19 items)

Wikidata's "video game" class includes subclasses the fetch picked up. Four items are not games at all: two adult DLC items from "Hentai Area 51" (an adult patch and a soundtrack), "BOOK OF HOURS + Soundtrack bundle", and a game demo. The other 15 are expansion packs (the 4 Cultist Simulator DLCs, Rome: Total War: Alexander), bundles, editions, a compilation, a total-conversion mod, an abandoned project (*Agent*) and a fan remake that doesn't exist (*Fallout: New Vegas Remake*).
Flags: `not-a-standalone-game`, `REMOVE`. Source: each row's Wikidata link, under P31 "instance of".

### 2. Duplicate editions (2)

- *Ghost of Tsushima Director's Cut* ([Q107398164](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q107398164)) duplicates [Q42564798](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q42564798).
- *Live A Live* appears twice: the 1994 original and the 2022 remake. Both are legitimate games, but on the map they read as a duplicate. Decide whether remakes count as separate entries.

### 3. Curated entries that are wrong (2) or have broken links (2)

Fourteen more curated entries are unverified: the Wikipedia article doesn't confirm the years, places or level lists. They're mostly level and track lists (Gran Turismo 7, F1 23, the Mario Kart city tracks, Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2 levels, Call of Duty 2 missions, Tomb Raider), plus the start and end years of Civilization VI and Hearts of Iron IV. Each one's note in the CSV says what's missing. The curated coordinates have no sources at all. The feasibility doc recommended a `source` and `confidence` on every placement, and I didn't implement that.

### 4. Sports games stored as story settings (64)

FIFA, PES, UEFA Euro, Madden, NHL, Olympics and F1 games are pinned at the host country (often coarsely), with the tournament year as their period. They should be `arena` placements, ideally at stadiums or circuits, or excluded. Flag: `sports-as-setting`, detected by Wikidata genre (P136) being a subclass of sports video game.

### 5. Vague time periods (87) and very long spans (89)

- 64 games carry only "ancient history" (4000 BCE–500 CE). The others with vague periods have prehistory, future, near future, Middle Ages, present day and similar.
- Some of these vague periods are hiding precise ones. *Egypt 1156 B.C.* has its year in its own title. *Die Kathedrale* is tagged "ancient history" but pinned at Germany. These look like Wikidata errors, but I haven't verified them.
- The dates for 12 vague periods come from my own guesses in `data/overrides.yaml` (for example, "future" = 2030–3000). They're unsourced and need your judgment.

### 6. Modern countries for historical settings (48)

Ancient China is pinned at the People's Republic of China, Mesopotamia at Iraq (*Age of Empires*), Ancient Greece at modern Greece. Coordinates of present-day states drift from where historical settings actually were. Flag: `modern-state-for-historical-setting`. The check is a heuristic: modern state and every span before 1900.

### 7. Coarse pins dominate (229 of 400 games)

More than half of all games have only country-, continent- or empire-level pins. This is the biggest UX limit, and it comes from how detailed Wikidata is, not from bugs. Fixing it means curating more precise places.

## How long-span games could work

The current rule ("a game matches if any span overlaps the range") is right for games set at fixed dates. It fails for the 89 games spanning more than 500 years, which match almost any window and crowd out specific results. These games tie places to time in different ways, so one field can't describe them all. I'd add a `time` type per span:

| Type | Example | How it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `fixed` | Vice City 1986, AC II 1476 | Solid pin, as now |
| `era` | "Edo period", "Kamakura period" | Matches on overlap; shown as approximate in time when the span is much wider than the selected window |
| `sandbox` | Civilization, EU4, Caesar III, Age of Empires | Time is a game mechanic, not the setting. Kept off the pin layer by default and listed separately ("Covers this era"), or shown as a region |
| `mythic` | Apotheon, Hercules, Age of Mythology | Mapped to a nominal era for filtering, labeled as myth |
| `vague` | "future", "prehistory", "present day" | Hidden by default behind a toggle |

This mirrors the place handling that already works. Hollow pins mean "approximate in space"; the same visual idea (fainter or dashed) would mean "approximate in time". **My recommendation:** add the `time` type plus one rule. Any span wider than about 5× the selected window renders as approximate, and `sandbox` and `vague` are hidden by default.

## Code issues

| # | Where | Issue | Impact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | `scripts/fetch-wikidata.ts:7` | `wdt:P31/wdt:P279* wd:Q7889` includes DLC, bundles, editions, demos, mods and unfinished projects | Data issue 1. Fix: exclude those classes, or require `P31 = Q7889` directly |
| C2 | `scripts/lib/periods.ts:49` | End dates stored as the first day of the next century are treated as inclusive, so the 9th century becomes 801–901 | 4 games (AC Mirage, AC Valhalla, Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time, Chronicles of the Sword); Victorian era ends 1901 |
| C3 | `scripts/lib/periods.ts:45-50` | A period with a start and no end becomes a single year | "contemporary history" and "2019–2020 Hong Kong protests" collapse to their start year |
| C4 | `scripts/fetch-wikidata.ts:66` | When a period has several P580/P585 values, the first row SPARQL returns wins. Row order isn't guaranteed | Refreshing the snapshot can change dates with no data change on Wikidata |
| C5 | `scripts/lib/dataset.ts:68-69` | Every Wikidata placement gets `kind: 'setting'` | Data issue 4. Genre (P136) is available to classify sports games as `arena` |
| C6 | `scripts/lib/dataset.ts:94` | The build fails unless there are exactly 400 games | Any cleanup (removing the 19 non-games) breaks the build until replacements are added. Use a minimum, or report the count without failing |
| C7 | `scripts/lib/dataset.ts:57` | A `release` override is silently skipped when the game has no release year | The game may lose its only span and be dropped with only a console warning |
| C8 | `scripts/lib/dataset.ts:77` | Curated Wikipedia links are guessed from the title | 2 broken links. Store full URLs, plus an offline link-check script |
| C9 | `src/types.ts`, `data/curated.yaml` | No per-placement `source` or `confidence`, though the feasibility doc called for it | Curated, imported and guessed data look the same in the UI |
| C10 | `src/filter.ts:16` | Overlap-only matching | Long-span section above |
| C11 | `src/map.ts` (regions layer) | Strategy-map bboxes are drawn but can't be clicked, and world-sized boxes stack their opacity | Minor |
| C12 | `src/map.ts:114` | Cluster popups take the first 200 leaves and show 30 | Fine for now; a stacked "United States" pin could exceed this as data grows |
| C13 | repo | The 11 Playwright browser checks used for verification live outside the repo; Netlify runs unit tests only | UI regressions go unnoticed |

None of the code issues is a security problem. Text from Wikidata is HTML-escaped in popups and the list, and there are no secrets or backend.

## Suggested order

1. **Delete the 19 non-game items and 1–2 duplicate editions, and make the game count non-blocking (C1, C6).** Mechanical, and it removes the most embarrassing rows.
2. **Fix the 4 curated errors and the date-parsing bugs (C2, C3).**
3. **Decide on long-span handling.** This one is yours: it changes what the atlas shows.
4. **Reclassify sports games (C5), then add `source` and `confidence` (C9) before curating more.**
